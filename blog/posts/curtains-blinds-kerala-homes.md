Windows in a Kerala home have a hard job. They let in bright afternoon sun for most of the year, face driving rain for months during the monsoon, and often look straight onto a neighbour's house or a busy road. The right curtains or blinds make a room cooler, calmer and more private. The wrong ones fade, sag or smell musty after one wet season.

Here is how we help customers choose at our showroom.

## Start with what the window needs to do

Before looking at colours, ask three simple questions about each window:

- **How much light do you want to block?** A bedroom may need near darkness for an afternoon nap, while a living room usually wants soft, filtered daylight.
- **How much privacy do you need, and when?** Some windows need privacy only at night; others face the road all day.
- **How much moisture reaches it?** Windows in kitchens, bathrooms and work areas, and anything near an open sit-out, see far more damp than a first-floor bedroom.

The answers usually point clearly to either curtains, blinds or a mix of both.

## Curtains: soft, warm and flexible

Fabric curtains are still the favourite for living rooms and bedrooms because they soften a room full of hard tiles and plaster. A few points make them work well here:

- **Choose the fabric for the climate.** Polyester and polyester blends tend to hold their shape, resist shrinking and dry faster than pure cotton or linen, which matters in a humid season. Natural fabrics look lovely but need more care.
- **Use a lining where the sun is strong.** A lining or blackout layer protects the main fabric from fading and helps keep the room cooler. It is especially useful on west-facing windows.
- **Pair a sheer with a main curtain.** A light sheer gives daytime privacy while letting in daylight; the heavier curtain closes at night.
- **Pick the right heading.** Eyelet curtains are simple and easy to slide; ripple and M-track styles hang in even, modern folds that suit contemporary homes.

Let curtains finish a little above the floor, rather than pooling on it, so they do not soak up water when floors are mopped or rain blows in.

## Blinds: neat, practical and easy to clean

Blinds take up less space and give a crisp, tidy look. They are a good choice for study rooms, offices, kitchens and any window where furniture sits close to the glass.

- **Zebra blinds** have alternating sheer and solid bands, so you can move from filtered light to privacy with one pull. They are our most popular choice for homes and offices.
- **Roman blinds** are fabric blinds that fold into soft pleats when raised. They bring the warmth of a curtain to a smaller window.
- **Roller, venetian and vertical blinds** suit workspaces, clinics and wide windows where you want clean lines and easy control.

In kitchens and work areas, choose a blind with a wipeable finish and keep it away from the hob. A blind that can be pulled fully up during cooking stays cleaner for longer.

## Sit-outs, balconies and the monsoon

Kerala homes spend a lot of life on the verandah and balcony, and these are the spaces that suffer most in the rain. **PVC outdoor blinds** are made for this. Lowered during a downpour, they keep rain off the sit-out and furniture; raised on a clear day, they disappear. They can also cut glare in the late afternoon.

For windows and doors that stay open in the evenings, **mosquito nets** let air move through the house without letting insects in. Sliding-door types work well for balcony doors.

## Caring for window coverings in humid months

- **Keep air moving.** Open curtains and blinds during dry spells so fabric and windows can breathe. Closed, damp fabric against cold glass is where musty smells begin.
- **Shake and vacuum regularly.** Dust holds moisture. A quick vacuum with a brush attachment keeps fabric fresher.
- **Wash or dry-clean as the fabric needs.** Follow the care advice for each fabric, and make sure curtains are completely dry before rehanging.
- **Fix the source of damp first.** If a window frame leaks or a wall shows seepage, have it repaired before new curtains go up, or the problem will return.
